Five steps.
One pot.

ROT burns dead coins, counts them in public and splits one fee pot by those counts. This is what it does and what it doesn't.

  1. Find your dead bags

    ROT reads the token accounts in your wallet and checks each token against public market data. Tokens with no pair, almost no liquidity or no recent trades are marked as looking dead.

  2. Burn them

    You burn the ones you choose from your own wallet. ROT builds the transaction and shows every mint first. It contains burn and close-account instructions and nothing else.

  3. The ledger finds it

    Every burn through the bin carries the public bin address. ROT reads that address, checks each burn on-chain and lists it with the reason it counted or didn't.

  4. The round closes

    Each round lasts days. When it ends, ROT publishes the points table. For hours anyone can point at a burn that shouldn't count.

  5. The pot buys $ROT

    The pot wallet holds the fees $ROT's trading creates. After the challenge window, it buys $ROT on the market and sends it to the wallets that burned, divided by points. Every buy and every transfer is a public transaction on the ledger.

One rule

100% of $ROT's creator fees go to the pot wallet. Nobody takes a cut. It was set at launch and locked, so nobody can change it, us included. Every round the pot buys $ROT and sends it to the wallets that burned.

Check the pot wallet

The rule will be written here before the first round, not after.

One point per token

Burning ten of the same token is still one point. Ten different dead tokens is ten.

Looks dead

A token counts only if the market data says it's dead: no pair, liquidity under USD, or no trades in 24 hours.

Had to exist

The token must have had a trading pair at least days before you burned it. Tokens that were never listed don't count.

Not your own

Tokens your wallet created or controls don't count.

Round cap

At most tokens per wallet in each round. This keeps one wallet from flooding the heap.

Paid in $ROT

Payouts are $ROT, bought on the market with the pot. The site shows what was paid, not what it will be worth.

Minimum payout

A wallet is paid only when its share is worth at least SOL. Smaller shares aren't sent, and their points carry into the next round.

Account costs

If your wallet has never held $ROT, the project opens the token account. That cost comes out of the pot, not your share.

Published, not hidden

The ledger, the rounds and the rules for counting are public files in the repo. If a rule changes, it changes in public.

Be honest about the risks.

A token that looks dead can come back, and burning it is permanent. Someone can also mint a worthless token and burn it for points. The listing check, the age check, the creator check, the round cap and a public blocklist are the defenses, and none of them is perfect. Payouts are $ROT, and what it's worth can fall after you receive it. The pot buys on a public market, so the buy has slippage and is itself market activity. The project runs the buy and the transfers from the pot wallet. They aren't automatic, and you're trusting us to run them. Every one is a public transaction you can check. Fees can be small or zero, and ROT isn't a promise that anything pays out or financial advice.
